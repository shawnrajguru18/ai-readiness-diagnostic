# Legal, Security, and Compliance Review Summary

## Executive Summary 

During planning and approval of the Product AI Agent Discovery Interview initiative, Product's Risk, Compliance, Security, and Legal stakeholders raised significant concerns regarding the use of a third-party AI interview platform (Superintelligent) to conduct employee interviews and generate analysis used in DXC's AI Use Case Evaluation engagement. These concerns centered on ownership of data and outputs, AI model training, security controls, regulatory compliance, third-party risk management, data retention, and contractual protections. DXC Legal subsequently conducted its own review and required additional contractual, supplier management, and governance activities before proceeding. 

## 1. Client Legal Requirements 

**Data Ownership and Intellectual Property**

Product required explicit confirmation that it would retain ownership of: 

- All interview responses and recordings. 

- Interview transcripts. 

- AI-generated outputs. 

- Derived insights and analysis. 

Any resulting intellectual property generated through the interview process. 

The client viewed interview content as proprietary business information and required contractual protection of all inputs, outputs, and derivative works generated during the engagement. 

**AI Model Training Restrictions**

Product required contractual assurance that: 

- Interview content could not be used to train foundation models. 

- Customer data could not be incorporated into future model improvements. 

- No AI training could occur without prior written authorization. 

This was identified as a critical requirement due to the sensitive and non-public nature of information expected to be discussed during interviews. 

**Data Destruction Rights**

Product required the right to: 

- Request deletion of all collected interview information. 

- Require deletion from DXC systems and any subcontractor systems. 

- Receive confirmation or certification that data was destroyed. 

The client specifically requested evidence that all copies, including those maintained by third-party providers, could be removed upon request. 

**Confidential Information Handling**

Product required all interview information to be treated as confidential information under the Master Services Agreement, including: 

- Voice recordings. 

- Interview transcripts. 

- Observations and findings. 

- Generated outputs. 

The concern was that sensitive operational, financial, and strategic information would likely be disclosed during interviews and therefore required contractual protection. 

**Liability and Indemnification**

Product sought language that would make DXC responsible for issues arising from use of the AI interview platform, including third-party claims associated with the technology provider. DXC Legal reviewed these provisions as part of contract negotiations and amendment discussions. 

## 2. Client Security Requirements

**Encryption Requirements**

Product requested documentation and confirmation regarding: 

- Encryption of data in transit. 

- Encryption of stored data. 

- Security controls protecting uploaded information. 

- Security architecture of the interview platform. 

These questions originated from the client's Risk and Compliance organization. 

**Hosting and Data Residency** 

The client requested clarification regarding: 

- Hosting location. 

- Physical and logical storage location of information. 

- Whether data would leave the United States. 

- Replication or backup activities outside U.S. boundaries. 

Data residency was considered a significant concern due to the nature of information being collected. 

**Security Certifications** 

Product requested evidence of: 

- ISO 27001 alignment or certification. 

- SOC reports. 

- Independent security validation. 

- Information security program maturity. 

The client sought objective evidence that the platform met accepted enterprise security standards. 

**Vendor Risk Management**

Because interviews were conducted using a third-party platform, Product wanted greater clarity regarding: 

- Ownership of the platform. 

- Platform architecture. 

- Security controls. 

- Operational governance. 

Reliance on external AI providers. 

This effectively became a third-party risk assessment exercise focused on the AI technology stack. 

## 3. AI Governance and Responsible AI Concerns 

**Model Transparency** 

Product asked specifically: 

- Which large language model was being used. 

- Which version of the model was being used. 

- Whether the solution was proprietary or based on OpenAI technology. 

This was intended to better understand potential risks associated with the supporting AI technology. 

**Bias, Drift, and Output Reliability** 

The Risk and Compliance team requested information regarding: 

- Model drift management. 

- Output validation. 

- Bias controls. 

- Quality assurance mechanisms. 

The client wanted assurance that interview results and generated recommendations would remain consistent, reliable, and auditable. 

**Explainability and Governance** 

The client sought greater confidence that recommendations produced by the platform would be generated through a controlled, repeatable process rather than unrestricted AI interactions. This concern drove discussions about interview structures, scoring methods, and analysis controls. 

## 4. Internal DXC Legal and Compliance Concerns 

DXC Legal raised additional concerns beyond those identified by Product. 

**Third-Party Contract Risk**

- DXC Legal identified that: 

- Superintelligent was performing part of the solution delivery. 

- No supplier agreement had yet been executed. 

Contractual commitments made to Product would need to be mirrored through supplier agreements. 

Legal recommended involvement from: 

Supply Chain Management. 

Legal review processes. 

AI governance review processes. 

**Definition of Deliverables** 

Legal questioned whether the client might expect ownership of: 

- AI interview outputs. 

- Work products generated by the AI system. 

- Supporting artifacts. 

This appeared to create potential conflicts with existing intellectual property provisions in the Product Master Agreement and required resolution through contract language. 

## 5. Additional Security Controls Adopted During the Project 

An internal DXC project review identified concerns regarding interview participants uploading potentially sensitive documents directly into the AI platform. As a result, the project team implemented several controls: 

**Document Upload Restrictions** 

The team decided to: 

- Prohibit direct document uploads into the AI interview tool. 

- Route documentation through approved sharing locations. 

- Require documents to be managed through designated client coordinators. 

This was done specifically to address security and compliance concerns. 

**Communication Updates**

The project team updated: 

- Interview communications. 

- Presentation materials. 

- Participant instructions. 

Participants were directed not to upload documents into the AI platform. 

**Regulatory Considerations** 

Internal discussions also identified concerns around: 

- GDPR requirements. 

- PII handling. 

- Data sovereignty. 

- Sensitive financial information. 

- Regional data-processing obligations. 

These topics were identified as future evaluation criteria for AI opportunities discovered during the engagement. 

## Overall Conclusion 

Product's concerns were not primarily about the concept of AI-driven interviews. Instead, the client's focus was on enterprise governance and risk management. The required safeguards fell into six major areas: 

- Ownership of all interview data, outputs, and derivative insights. 

- Prohibition on use of client data for AI model training. 

- Strong security controls, encryption, and U.S.-based data residency. 

- Formal data destruction and retention procedures. 

- Third-party AI vendor risk management and contractual controls. 

- Governance mechanisms to address model bias, drift, explainability, compliance, and regulatory obligations.
